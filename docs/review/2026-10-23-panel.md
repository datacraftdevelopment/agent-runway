# Design review: Astra and Fable panel (2026-10-23)

Two-seat adversarial review through Ringer's review panel: Astra (Codex, gpt-6-astra, 124 s) and Fable (Claude, claude-fable-5, 264 s). Input: `docs/review/2026-10-23-packet.md` and `docs/concept.md`. Astra: 6 blockers, 5 should-fix. Fable: 4 blockers, 7 should-fix, 4 notes. Both passed the kit's validator first time.

## Verdict

The shape holds (one controller, Herdr on the workers, receipts, one gate in code). The design had one hole, hit three times: nothing on a worker, in a cloud session or in a session hook could write to the controller's store, yet the contract gave all three writes there. And the gate read its inputs from places the builder could rewrite.

## Rulings (agreed by both seats unless noted; reversible by Joe)

1. **Only the controller claims.** It writes the claim (with a dispatch id and attempt id) into its own journal before it starts any session, local or cloud. A session checks that the claim names it and aborts if not. No session ever self-claims.
2. **Receipts travel by outbox and pull.** A worker's `receipt` (and the session hook) appends to a local outbox outside the worktree. The controller pulls it over the SSH link it already holds, on each Herdr state change and on a slow poll, dedupes on event ids, and acknowledges. No route back, no listener, no daemon.
3. **One authority per kind of fact.** Ticket definitions travel by git in each repo. The controller's journal is the only authority for run state (claims, receipts, verdicts, decisions). Herdr is advisory: a `done` or `unknown` with no closing receipt after a grace period becomes a synthetic `failed` and a needs-you card, never success.
4. **Leases for orphaned claims.** Each claim records its Herdr session; when that session is gone, the controller fails the attempt and offers a fresh dispatch. (Fable)
5. **Cloud sessions** get a dispatch id at launch and report through structured result files on their pushed branch; a deadline turns silence into `failed`. If a cloud provider can't carry the dispatch id, it is out of v1.
6. **The gate trusts nothing the builder can edit.** Risk is captured in the controller's journal at filing time (plus reviewer raises). `runway.json` is read from the default branch. Ticket files, `runway.json` and CI files are sensitive paths by default. "Checks green" means CI on the head commit or a check the gate runs itself; pasted output is diagnostics only.
7. **The controller owns the whole hand-off** (Astra's blocker, Fable's notes): dispatch, review launch on a different model pinned to the head, one fix pass then a fresh review, gate, mark ready, merge with an expected-head condition, record the merge commit. Typed receipt schemas; build done is not ticket done.
8. **Ringer is out of the core loop.** Review seats call `claude -p` / `codex exec` directly; no PATH sniffing. Ringer stays only as an opt-in per-ticket worker strategy (`Strategy: swarm`); if it's missing the ticket is `blocked`, never silently downgraded. Not in Monday.
9. **Protocol operations are preauthorized** (claim check, outbox writes, worktree under the workspace root, push to the ticket branch, draft PR). Anything else outside the worktree stays needs-you. (Astra)
10. **Decisions have one writer: the controller.** Any Mac can open the controller's board and answer, but the answer is written once by the controller, with a decision id and revision, and a worker can never answer its own needs-you. (Astra; reconciles Joe's "answer from any app")
11. **For strangers:** SSH reachable is required, Tailscale recommended. Setup tests a real non-interactive `claude -p` / `codex exec` over SSH and the ssh-agent key. "Joe's go" becomes "the operator's go". With only one model, review runs as a fresh same-model session and the gate caps the result at needs-you. v1 is macOS. (Fable for the one-model fallback; Astra would require two models in the fleet.)
12. **Subscription terms** for unattended use are checked before setup ships; API-key mode is the documented fallback. (Fable)

## Monday

Both seats agree on what to leave out: setup wizard, board polish, session hook, cloud, Ringer. They split on the loop: Astra wants a real SSH worker plus fault drills; Fable keeps it on the controller with a 30-minute SSH sign-in spike first. Combined plan:

1. Spike (30 min, go/no-go): `claude -p` and `codex exec` in a non-interactive SSH session on one worker, through Herdr.
2. Loop: one practice repo, one low-risk ticket. Controller claims, starts the worker through Herdr, pulls the outbox, launches the other-model review on the head, gates, merges with an expected head.
3. Stretch: drop the SSH link mid-run, replay a receipt, try a second claim, push a new head after review and check the merge is refused.
